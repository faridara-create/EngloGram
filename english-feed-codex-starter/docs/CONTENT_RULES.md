# Content Generation Rules

## Lesson composition
Every lesson:
- one topic
- one coherent subtopic
- CEFR target: C1 unless explicitly overridden
- exactly 10 new learning items
- approximately 5 single-word vocabulary items and 5 collocations
- exact balance may vary only when justified by topic quality, but target remains 50/50

## Item selection
Prefer:
- high-utility C1 vocabulary
- natural collocations
- language that transfers into speaking and writing
- phrases a native speaker would actually use

Avoid:
- obscure dictionary curiosities
- archaic vocabulary
- excessively technical terminology unless the lesson topic requires it
- transparent cognates as the majority of a lesson
- repeated items already introduced as NEW in previous lessons

## Definitions
- English only
- concise
- learner-friendly but not childish
- must uniquely identify the answer within the lesson

## German translations
- natural German, not word-for-word
- give 1–3 useful equivalents where needed
- collocations should be translated idiomatically

## IPA
- consistent British English IPA
- validate if possible
- do not invent IPA by naive spelling rules

## Examples
Exactly 3 per item.
Each:
- natural C1-level English
- useful context
- 12–30 words preferred
- varied grammar and discourse function
- German translation directly paired
- should not all begin with the target item
- should avoid repetitive project-management templates across lessons

## Recap story
- use all 10 items naturally
- tell or explain something meaningful
- should not read like 10 example sentences stitched together
- coherent setting, argument, narrative, case, explanation, or mini-essay
- target 250–450 English words
- split into pages for mobile readability
- every introduced item must appear at least once
- inflected/grammatical variants allowed where natural
- do not distort language just to force an exact surface form

## Quiz
10 definition-to-term questions.
For each:
- correct answer = one of lesson's 10 items
- 4 distractors = other items from same lesson
- definition must clearly distinguish the correct item
- randomize option order at runtime, not necessarily in source data

## Duplicate policy
Maintain a global registry.
A term may:
- be NEW once
- reappear later in examples/story/review
- never silently become NEW again

Near-duplicate collocations should be detected, e.g.
- raise concerns
- raise a concern
may belong to the same lexical family.

## Topic coherence
A lesson is not a random list under a broad category.
Example:
Business is too broad.
Good subtopics:
- Meetings & Influence
- Handling Conflict
- Presenting a Recommendation
- Negotiating Scope
- Executive Communication
